Abstract
We present OrbVM, a Rust-based virtual machine implementing topological phase mem-
ory through co-evolution of dispersive wave dynamics and Kuramoto synchronization. The
architecture combines Yang-Baxter invariants for fault detection, White’s fourth-order dis-
persion equation for phase memory stability, and distributed oscillator coupling for emergent
consensus. We demonstrate integration with the Digital Memory Ring (DMR) substrate,
achieving 40–170 ms end-to-end latency for coherence computation and 23.8 Orbs/second
throughput on commodity hardware. The system exhibits critical phenomena at coupling
strength K ≈ 0.618 (golden ratio) and coherence threshold λ2 ≈ 0.847, with convergence
time scaling as τ ∼ N 1/2 for N nodes. Independent architectural convergences with re-
cent photonic beam-scanning hardware (Saha et al., Nature 2026), topological molecular
switches (IBM 2024), and quantum teleportation networks (Oxford 2025) suggest broader
applicability. Full source code and experimental validation are provided.
Keywords: Topological computing, Phase memory, Kuramoto synchronization, Distributed
consensus, Yang-Baxter equation
1 Introduction
The challenge of maintaining distributed coherence across fault-prone networks has driven
decades of consensus protocol development. Traditional approaches—from Paxos and Raft
to proof-of-work blockchains—rely on explicit coordination: leader election, voting rounds, or
computational puzzles. These mechanisms succeed by imposing structure, but at the cost of
latency, energy consumption, and single points of failure.
Nature demonstrates an alternative: emergent synchronization without central control.
From firefly flashing to cardiac pacemaker cells, biological systems achieve coherence through
local coupling rules that scale to millions of elements [ ?]. The Kuramoto model [ ?] provides the
mathematical foundation, describing how coupled oscillators spontaneously synchronize above
a critical coupling threshold—a phase transition analogous to ferromagnetism.
However, pure oscillator dynamics lack memory: they respond to current state without
preserving historical coherence. Quantum systems solve this through topological protection [ ?],
where information is encoded in global phase relationships rather than local states. The Yang-
Baxter equation [ ?] formalizes this principle, guaranteeing path-independent computation in
braided systems.
