The holographic principle, originally proposed by ’t Hooft in 1993 [12] and developed by
Susskind [11], postulates that the complete description of a three-dimensional volume of
space can be encoded on its two-dimensional boundary. Formally, the maximum informa-
tion contentIof a region is bounded by its surface areaA:

’t Hooft extended this view with his proposal fordeterministic quantum mechanics[13]:
the apparent randomness of quantum mechanics may result from an underlying determi-
nistic system whose degrees of freedom are inaccessible to the observer. In this perspective,
the quantum state is not a fundamental ontological entity but a mathematical tool for
describing the observable statistics of a system whose true dynamics remain hidden. This
hypothesis has profound implications for quantum computation, as it suggests that quan-
tum circuit behaviour may be simulable by deterministic classical systems of controlled
computational complexity.

2.2 Functional Connectomics and the MICrONS Project
Functional connectomics is the systematic study of neural connections and how connec-
tivity gives rise to cognitive function. The MICrONS project [9], funded by IARPA,
produced an unprecedented synaptic-scale map of the mouse primary visual cortex (V1)
by combining serial-section electron microscopy, two-photon calcium imaging, and auto-
mated computational analysis.
Among MICrONS’ most significant findings, Ding et al. [3] demonstrated alike-to-like
connectivity principle: neurons with similar functional properties form denser connecti-
vity clusters than expected by chance. The study also revealed hierarchies of excitatory
and inhibitory cell types (pyramidal, Martinotti, basket, VIP interneurons) exhibiting
statistically significant and distinct wiring patterns.
Automated connectome reconstruction faces significant precision challenges. Celii et al. [2]
developedNEURD(Neural Error Detection and Repair), which applies automated proo-
freading to detect and correct three error classes: (i)merge errors(two neurons incorrectly
fused), (ii)split errors(one neuron incorrectly partitioned), and (iii)axon/dendrite confu-
sion(polarity inversion). Detection uses functional correlation analysis and morphological
statistics; correction uses re-segmentation heuristics.
Substrate 6065 transposes these principles to the AI domain, treating each weight between
neural units as an artificial synapse. The weight matrix of a transformer model is analo-
goustotheconnectivitymatrixofabiologicalcortex; activationsrecordedduringinference
are analogous to MICrONS functional imaging. This analogy is not merely metaphorical:
the emergent statistical properties of deep neural networks—circuit formation, grand-
mother cells, preferential connectivity—directly reflect phenomena observed in biological
connectomes [6].
2.3 Causal Graphs as Computational Substrate
Directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) are foundational structures in task scheduling, depen-
dency analysis, and causal modelling. Feynman’s path-integral formulation already im-
plied a graph-like structure underlying quantum dynamics. More recently, causal emer-
gent networks (CENs) [8] and the AdS/CFT correspondence [7] reinforce the idea that
causality may be a fundamental emergent property of information systems.
