Abstract. The integration of neuromorphic hardware with blockchain-based governance mech-
anisms represents a frontier in trustless computing architecture. This paper introduces the
Aurum Grid Initiative , a strategic roadmap that scales the Aletheia Protocol from smart
contract auditing to AGI-native systems through three coordinated technological missions: neu-
romorphic computation with memristive plasticity (Mission I), advanced cryptographic custody
via FROST threshold signatures and real-world asset (R W A) traceability (Mission II), and univer-
sal archival with geopolitical settlement applications (Mission III). We formalize the Dissipation
( D) metrica quantiable measure of cognitive coherence grounded in information theoryand
demonstrate its applicability across neuromorphic hardware, cryptographic protocols, and cross-
chain nancial settlement infrastructure. Incorporating updated evidence on Intel Loihi 2 and the
Hala Point system (1.15 billion neurons), recent advances in SrTiO 3-based memristive synapses,
FROST/RFC 9591 production readiness, LayerZero V2 omnichain deployments (165+ chains,
$87B in tokenized assets), and Arweave permanent storage, we validate three core hypotheses
with strengthened empirical support. We also introduce the Multi-V erier Security require-
ment derived from the April 2026 LayerZero protocol update and present a quantitative 36-month
implementation roadmap with formal success criteria.

2. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
2.1. The Dissipation Metric
Drawing on Shannon's information theory [16],
we dene the Dissipation ( D) metric as a mea-
sure of entropy loss in information processing
systems:
D = Hin − Hout − I(X; Y ), (1)
where Hin is the Shannon entropy of input sig-
nals, Hout is the entropy of output signals, and
I(X; Y ) is the mutual information between in-
put and output.

 Neuromorphic context. Low D indicates
ecient semantic compression without infor-
mation losscoherent encoding by the SNN.
 Cryptographic context. Controlled D en-
sures protocol robustness; anomalous growth
ags adversarial manipulation or key-share
compromise.
 Financial settlement. D < Dmax conrms
transactional integrity across chain bound-

3.2. Memristive Plasticity and the D Cou-
pling
Recent work demonstrates that SrTiO 3 (STO)-
based memristive devices inherently emulate all
six key synaptic plasticity mechanisms in a sin-

(STDP), metaplasticity, weight multiplication,
and forgetting [79]. These devices achieve
energy consumption approximately two orders
of magnitude below GPU implementations for
equivalent neural network functionality [7, 17].
The coupling hypothesis for the Aletheia Pro-
tocol is:
∆Gmem ∝ ∆DSNN, (2)
where ∆Gmem is the change in memristor con-
ductance (measurable via four-terminal sens-
ing) and ∆DSNN is the corresponding change
in Dissipation of the hosted SNN. Equation (2)
provides the falsiable prediction for Phase 2
validation (Section 3.3).

4. MISSION II: CRYPTOGRAPHIC

4.1. FROST Threshold Architecture
The FROST protocol [10, 11] improves upon
prior Schnorr threshold schemes by: (i) elimi-
nating the concurrency restriction (signing can
proceed in parallel without compromising secu-
rity), (ii) employing a binding technique that
prevents forgery under concurrent sessions, and
(iii) reducing network overhead to two rounds
of communication.
We specify a 3-of-5 FROST conguration
for institutional asset custody:
Parameter V alue
Signing participants 5 (geographically distributed)
Threshold 3 valid shares required
Key generation DKG per RFC 9591 [11, 18]
Preprocessing Non-interactive async signing
Signature size 64 bytes (Taproot compatible)
Security assumption Discrete log over Schnorr groups
Single Point of Failure Elimination. Un-
der the discrete log assumption, an adversary
controlling fewer than three participants can-
not forge a valid signature [10]. Any three
participants can independently verify message
signature pairs.

8. RISK ANALYSIS AND MITIGA-

8.1. T echnical Risks
R1: Memristor conductance variability.
STO-based memristors exhibit device-to-device
and cycle-to-cycle variations that may reduce
the D-conductance correlation below the r >
0.85 target [9, 20]. Mitigation: parallel de-
velopment of information-theoretic coherence
bounds as fallback metrics; early empirical val-
idation in Phase 1 with contingency for frame-
work pivot.
R2: FROST signing latency. Geograph-
ically distributed signers may introduce la-
tency incompatible with high-frequency settle-
ment. Mitigation: preprocessing nonces gener-
ated asynchronously; target 200 ms for 3-of-5
cong; 2-of-3 emergency liquidation path.
R3: LayerZero bridge security. Post-April
2026, multi-DVN is mandatory; single-DVN
congurations are rejected by the protocol [13].
The Aurum Grid architecture is designed with
multi-DVN from inception. Mitigation: com-
prehensive security audit (OpenZeppelin/Trail
of Bits); time-locked withdrawal mechanisms;
insolvency insurance fund.
8.2. Regulatory Risks
R4: BRICS-Pay sanctions exposure.
Western sanctions may target BCBPI interop-
erability nodes. Mitigation: oine-capable ar-
chitecture; open-source code enabling indepen-
dent implementations; distributed validator set
across jurisdictions [19].
R5: Arweave/GDPR conict. Immutable

provisions. Mitigation: zero-knowledge com-

sure; territorial segmentation of Arweave stor-
